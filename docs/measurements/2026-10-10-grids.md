# Grid block: live check of the five grid questions (2026-10-10)

Suite: [evals/grids-v1.json](../../evals/grids-v1.json). Its retrieval half runs in
`tests/test_grids_eval_suite.py`. The live half is these runs, read page by page.

Setup: `scripts/live_check.py ask`, which reads the owner's `.env` inside the
process (OpenAI provider, model `gpt-5.6-luna`, `DEADBOT_OPENAI_REASONING_EFFORT=low`),
then forces the SQLite store and turns the response cache off. Costs are the
`turn_metrics` lines, laid out with `scripts/measure_turns.table`. The five cases
ran in parallel.

1. **Dark Star vs Playing in the Band.** A comparison of two song_overview
   units with their year charts, era units for 1968–70, 1971–74 and 1976–82, and
   six performances to hear. The suite accepts a chart or a grid here. The
   counts quoted (70 in 1969, 82 in 1972) are performance counts from
   aggregate_data, which matches the charts. It is a good page without a grid.
2. **Europe '72 tour.** `query_catalog` with `tour: "Europe '72"` came back
   with no rows and no hint (the library calls it "Europe 1972"). The model
   fell back to SQL and then wrote the route as four editorial timelines plus a
   fact_grid. There is no show card, no grid and no player: the visitor can
   read about 22 nights but hear none of them.
3. **Spring '77 Scarlet > Fire.** `get_segue_pairing`, then a 14-row
   version_strip that plays each night's pair. The lengths are tape lengths
   from the tool, not invented, and April 25 is called out as untimed. The
   palette's pairing component fits a pairing question, so this is a good page
   without a grid. It counts "Spring '77" by calendar year (from March 18) and
   stops at May 25. The library's Spring 1977 tour runs April 22 to June 9, and
   June 7 has the pair.
4. **Other One / Dark Star.** The model read setlists through SQL and wrote
   four timeline blocks: "The Other One dominated the early and late ends of
   the tour, while Dark Star repeatedly stepped into its place in the middle."
   That is wrong. The two songs alternate night by night from April 7 to May 26
   (The Other One on 12 nights, Dark Star on 11). It did catch Bickershaw. A
   grid shows the alternation at a glance, and its absence is what let the
   prose drift.
5. **Most played in 1977.** A ranked_list of distinct shows with Drums and
   Space excluded (Estimated Prophet 51 of 60), plus a fact_grid on when in the
   year. No grid, which is right.

### What the model was missing

- **The grid was not in the component palette.** The composing section lists
  every standalone component the model chooses from (data_chart, ranked_list,
  version_strip and the rest) but not the grid. The grid appeared only once, in
  "Well-worn routes", in abstract terms ("how two lists meet") that the model
  did not connect to "tell me about a tour" or "how did two songs trade
  places". One sentence also undercut it: "a grid of the same songs already
  given their own units" (written about fact_grid) told the model to end the
  page rather than add a grid.
- **Tour names.** Fans say "Europe '72" and "Spring '77". The library says
  "Europe 1972" and "Spring 1977". `get_grid` already answers a miss with
  `closest_tours`, but `query_catalog`, where the model looks first, returned
  an empty list with no hint.

## Change 1: the prompt (deadbot/graph.py)

These fix the whole family: a tour, a run or a handful of nights read through
its songs, and songs compared year by year. Neither is a keyword route.

- The "Well-worn routes" sentence now describes those tasks in a fan's terms,
  with examples taken from outside the suite ("which nights carried Playing in
  the Band, how long each Eyes of the World ran, where two jams shared a
  night"). It also names `get_grid`'s `tour` argument.
- The grid joins the palette paragraph next to version_strip, saying what it
  draws (nights × songs in minutes, songs × years in shows) and what the
  visitor does with it (hover for facts, play the nights).
- "a grid of the same songs" became "a fact_grid of the same songs", which is
  the component that sentence was always about.

All prompt text says what to do. `tests/test_graph.py` still passes.

## Change 2: body items written beside groups (deadbot/finish.py, deadbot/plan_stream.py)

`finish.loose_items_as_group` reads a top-level `items` list as one more
collection where it was written: before the groups if the list came first,
after them otherwise. `PlanStreamer._loose_items_closed` reads it the same way,
so the draft and the delivered page agree. This applies AGENTS.md's rules to
read the plan leniently and keep the model's work on the page. It is the same
kind of rescue as the existing "a body item written straight into groups"
rule. Test: `test_items_written_beside_groups_stand_as_one_more_group_in_the_stream_and_the_plan`.

Evidence: replaying run 2's recorded case-4 plan through
`build_experience_response` now delivers its grid (22 rows, four highlighted
cells, the model's coverage note) after its two editorial blocks.

## Change 3: tour nicknames in query_catalog (deadbot/tools.py)
