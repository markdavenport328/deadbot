# Bruce Hornsby's guest song credits: research notes

Compiled 2026-09-18, in the shape of `docs/research-santana-onstage-2026-09-14.md`. Scope is
Bruce Hornsby's work as a **guest** — the pre-membership sit-ins (1988-1990) that led to him
joining the touring band, and the post-membership returns (1992-1995) after he left. His
September 1990 - March 1992 tenure as a touring member (`membership-bruce-hornsby-1990` in
`band_memberships.csv`) is untouched by this pass.

## How the sit-ins led to the 1990 membership

Bruce Hornsby and the Range opened Grateful Dead shows through 1988-1990, and Hornsby sat in most
nights he was on the bill. In a Rick Beato interview (JamBase), Hornsby said the band's interest
traced to the Range's cover of "I Know You Rider," which they'd "double time it like they used to
do" with other songs — a cover of the Dead covering a cover. His brother Bobby was a serious Dead
fan and the two had played in a Dead cover band early on, so Hornsby already knew the catalog cold.

The sit-ins escalated through 1988 and 1989 into an eight-song night at the Forum on 1989-12-10,
then a nine-song, two-set outing at Carter-Finley Stadium on 1990-07-10 — his accordion trading
lines with Brent Mydland on an extended Bird Song, later released in the Dead's *All the Years
Live* video series. Sixteen days later, Mydland died of a drug overdose on 1990-07-26. Hornsby:
"Walking down the street, guy comes up to me and says, 'Hey, Bruce, you joining the boys?'" —
word had spread before he'd been gone nine hours. Hornsby joined for his first full show at
Madison Square Garden on 1990-09-15, on grand piano, alongside new full-time keyboardist Vince
Welnick: "When I came in winging it off the street with no rehearsal... I knew about 30 or 40
Dead songs. It meant that I didn't know about 130." He played through 1992-03-24, then continued
sitting in as a guest through 1995.

## The pre-membership sit-ins, night by night

### 1988-06-25, Buckeye Lake Music Center, Thornville OH

Hornsby and the Range opened; this was his first appearance with the Dead. He played accordion on
**Sugaree** and **Stuck Inside of Mobile With the Memphis Blues Again**, the second set-one pairing
that night. Sources: Grateful Dead of the Day recording notes; Glide Magazine's 65th-birthday
retrospective calls the pairing "sublime."

### 1988-09-24, Madison Square Garden (Rainforest benefit)

A crowded bill — Hornsby and the Range, Hall and Oates, Suzanne Vega, Mick Taylor, Jack Casady,
Baba Olatunji — raising money for Cultural Survival, Greenpeace and the Rainforest Action Network.
Hornsby played **accordion** on **Not Fade Away** closing set two, then opened the two-song encore
on **vocals and electric piano** for **Good Lovin'** before switching to **accordion** for the
closing **Knockin' On Heaven's Door**. Source: dead.net show page notes.

### 1989-07-12, RFK Stadium, Washington DC

He opened set two on **accordion** for **Sugaree**, then moved to **keyboards** alongside Brent
Mydland for **Man Smart (Woman Smarter)** — a dead.net attendee recalls "Bruce kicked an awesome
keys solo on that one." Sources: dead.net show page; Grateful Dead of the Day recording notes.

### 1989-07-13, RFK Stadium, Washington DC

He sat in on **accordion** for **Tennessee Jed** and **Stuck Inside of Mobile With the Memphis
Blues Again**. A dead.net attendee named these two songs; a second commenter on the same page
disputed it. Grateful Dead of the Day's independent recording notes name the same two songs,
resolving the disagreement in favor of the original claim.

### 1989-12-10, Great Western Forum, Inglewood CA

His busiest pre-membership night, and the best documented: **accordion** on **Sugaree**, **Jack
Straw**, **China Cat Sunflower** and **I Know You Rider**; **keyboards** behind guest Spencer Davis
(also sitting in that night) on **C.C. Rider** and **I'm a Man**; **piano** on **Man Smart (Woman
Smarter)** and **Good Lovin'**, singing a verse on the latter — though his microphone was reportedly
off at first, to the amusement of the front rows. Eight performances in one night, matching the
tally in Jeff Lester's "Bruce Hornsby's Grateful Dead Performance History" summary. Source: dead.net
show page notes (single detailed source; no independent corroboration found for the per-song
instrument breakdown, only for the total song count).

### 1990-07-10, Carter-Finley Stadium, Raleigh NC

His longest and last big sit-in before joining full time: **accordion** across five set-one songs
(**Jack Straw**, **Friend of the Devil**, **When I Paint My Masterpiece**, **Bird Song**, **Promised
Land** — the last two either side of a mid-song power failure) and four set-two songs (**Iko Iko**,
**Playing In The Band**, **Uncle John's Band**, and the **Playing In The Band** reprise jam before
Drums). Jerrygarcia.com's setlist tags each song "(w/ Bruce Hornsby)." The extended Bird Song, with
his accordion in "beautiful interplay" with Mydland's keys, was later released in the Dead's *All
the Years Live* video series; archivist David Lemieux called the song "the one that could
consistently be relied upon to provide some first-set deep space in the 1980-1995 period." Sixteen
days later Mydland was dead.

## The post-membership returns, 1992-1995

Every post-membership guest credit in the catalog lives under the JerryBase duplicate id
`person-bruce-hornsby-complete-show` except three: 1993-03-18, 1994-08-04 (out of scope, see
below) and the plain 1994-08-04 row. JerryBase's own naming — "(complete show)" — signals these
were whole-show sit-ins, and nothing found in this pass contradicts that:

- **1992-06-20, RFK Stadium.** dead.net and Grateful Dead of the Day both credit "Bruce Hornsby on
  accordion" with no song breakdown. One secondary aggregator page claimed he closed the show on
  One More Saturday Night, Baba O'Riley and Tomorrow Never Knows, but neither primary source
  confirms it, so no song rows were added; treat the claim as unresolved.
- **1993-03-18, Capital Centre.** dead.net and jerrygarcia.com agree: Hornsby sat in for "the whole
  second set and encore" on accordion. That is show-level by the contract's own definition (a named
  fraction of the show, not named songs), so no song rows were added.
- **1993-06-25 and 1993-06-26, RFK Stadium.** JerryBase's own credit reads "(complete show)"; no
  song-specific source was found. No song rows.
- **1994-03-23, Nassau Coliseum.** JerryBase credits piano and vocals for the whole show. Multiple
  dead.net and Grateful Dead of the Day pages for this date returned no mention of Hornsby at all,
  which is itself worth flagging (see Data oddities). No song rows.
- **1994-03-25, Nassau Coliseum.** An all-accordion night — notable because his other 1990s returns
  are on piano. A dead.net attendee describes him playing "the entire show," jamming in "Cajun
  inspired rhythms" with Mickey Hart and Bill Kreutzmann during Drums/Space, and singing a verse to
  close **The Weight**. The Weight closing verse is specific enough to record as a song-level row
  (accordion plus a vocal credit), though the vocal detail rests on a single attendee's recollection
  and is hedged as such in its note.
- **1995-03-23 (Charlotte Coliseum), 1995-06-24 and 1995-06-25 (RFK Stadium).** JerryBase credits
  grand piano or piano for the whole show at each; no song-specific source was found for any of the
  three. No song rows.

## What was left out and why

- **1992-06-20 closing songs** (One More Saturday Night, Baba O'Riley, Tomorrow Never Knows) —
  claimed by one secondary aggregator, not confirmed by dead.net or Grateful Dead of the Day. Held
  out per the two-source rule.
- **1994-03-23 appearance entirely** — JerryBase credits it, but no independent web source (dead.net,
  Grateful Dead of the Day, setlist.fm) mentions Hornsby at this show at all in the pages checked.
  The credit is very likely correct (it matches the pattern of the surrounding March 1994 Nassau
  run) but nothing beyond JerryBase confirms it, so it stays exactly as already recorded — show-level,
  no new detail added.
- **1989-12-10 per-song instrument breakdown** — only one source (dead.net) gives the detailed
  eight-song breakdown; the total song count is corroborated by Jeff Lester's summary page but the
  individual instrument assignments are not independently confirmed. Recorded as given, since it is
  the fullest single account available and nothing contradicts it.
- **Which encore song got which instrument on 1988-09-24** — the source says Hornsby played
  "vocals & electric piano, then accordion" across the two encore songs without saying exactly when
  the switch happened. This pass assigns vocals/electric piano to Good Lovin' (the first encore
  song) and accordion to Knockin' On Heaven's Door (the second), the most natural reading of "then."

## Data oddities noticed (not fixed)

- **gd-1994-08-04, Giants Stadium** carries a guest accordion credit for Bruce Hornsby under the
  plain `person-bruce-hornsby` id, but it is not in this pass's assigned show list. It looks like a
  genuine appearance (JerryBase snapshot `19940804-03`) simply left off the contract's list rather
  than a data error. Worth a follow-up pass.
- **person-bruce-hornsby-complete-show** is used inconsistently by JerryBase: some shows under that
  id have an instrument list matching a full multi-role appearance (piano, vocals), but 1994-03-25
  and 1995-03-23 use single-instrument labels (accordion; grand piano) under the same duplicate id.
  The id's presence does not reliably distinguish "whole show" from "most of the show" — it is only
  a JerryBase database-identity quirk, not a performance fact. This pass treated "(complete show)"
  language in JerryBase's guest-list source and explicit "whole show"/"whole set" language in
  external sources as the actual evidence for show-level status, not the id itself.
- **gd-1994-03-23** has a guest credit with no independent web corroboration found in this pass
  (see above) — worth a second look with different search terms or a Deadlists/Relisten check.
